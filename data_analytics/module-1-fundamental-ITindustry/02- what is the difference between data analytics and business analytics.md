# Data Analytics vs. Business Analytics

**Data analytics** is the broader process of collecting, preparing, exploring, and analyzing data to find patterns, answer questions, and support decisions. It can be applied in many fields, including healthcare, science, government, sports, and business.

**Business analytics** applies analytical methods specifically to business questions. It uses business data and context to help an organization understand performance, solve problems, identify opportunities, and make decisions.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Data analytics | Business analytics |
|---|---|---|
| **Main focus** | Working with data to discover and explain useful findings | Using analysis to address business goals and decisions |
| **Scope** | Broad; used across many industries and domains | Focused on organizations, customers, operations, finance, sales, and strategy |
| **Typical questions** | What patterns are in the data? Is there a change or relationship? | Why are sales falling? Which customers should we target? How can operations improve? |
| **Common data** | Any relevant data, such as scientific measurements, images, sensor readings, or transactions | Business data, such as sales, costs, customer behavior, inventory, and marketing results |
| **Typical outputs** | Analyses, visualizations, statistical findings, and models | Business recommendations, forecasts, plans, and performance insights |

## How They Relate

Business analytics is a business-focused application of data analytics. The two fields overlap: both can use statistics, data visualization, programming, and predictive models. The distinction is mainly the **purpose and context** of the analysis, not a completely different set of tools.

## Example

A retailer notices that revenue has decreased:

- A **data analyst** might examine transaction data, check data quality, and identify when and where sales changed.
- A **business analyst** might combine those findings with business context—such as pricing, promotions, stock availability, and customer segments—and recommend actions to improve revenue.

In practice, one person or team may perform both kinds of work.
