# LAN, MAN, and WAN

These terms describe computer networks by the geographic area they cover. The boundaries are approximate and can vary by context.

| Network | Full name | Typical coverage | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LAN** | Local Area Network | A small area, such as a home, office, school, or building | Devices connected to a home Wi-Fi router |
| **MAN** | Metropolitan Area Network | A city or large campus, connecting multiple LANs | A network linking an organization’s offices across a city |
| **WAN** | Wide Area Network | Large geographic areas, such as countries or continents | A company network connecting offices in different countries |

- **LAN** is usually privately managed and often provides high-speed connections over a limited area.
- **MAN** connects networks across a metropolitan area and may be operated by an organization or a service provider.
- **WAN** connects networks over long distances and often relies on telecommunications providers. The **internet** is the largest and best-known example of a WAN.

**In brief:** LAN covers a local area, MAN covers a city-sized area, and WAN covers a much wider geographic area.
